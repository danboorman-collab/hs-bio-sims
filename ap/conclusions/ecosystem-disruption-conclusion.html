What a conclusion has to do

  1. Make the claimAnswer the question the investigation asked, in one sentence, with no more certainty than your results carry. Where two error bars overlap or a test fails to reject, say what the data do not show rather than picking the higher number.
  2. Give the evidenceQuote the numbers from your own results that the claim rests on, with their units: a mean and its error bar, a statistic and its critical value, a five-number summary, a value read off a line. Not a number from the lesson, and not the method.
  3. Give the reasoningSay why that evidence supports that claim, and connect it to the biology. This is the step that turns a description of what happened into a conclusion about what it means.
