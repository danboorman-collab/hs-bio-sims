Eight questions

Energetics · Topic 2.7

On carrying this investigation out, and on what its results show. Each question is marked as soon as it is answered, and every option is explained.

Question 1 of 8

Six potato cores are cut from one potato, blotted, weighed, and left for an hour in sucrose solutions from 0.0 to 1.0 M. Each is then blotted again and reweighed.

Why is the percent change in mass worked out rather than the change in grams?

Question 2 of 8

A potato core weighed 4.80 g at the start and 4.32 g after an hour in sucrose solution.

What is its percent change in mass?

Question 3 of 8

A student finds that a best-fit line through six potato cores crosses zero change in mass at 0.36 M sucrose. The solutions were at 25 °C, the ionization constant of sucrose is 1, and R is 0.0831 L·bar/(mol·K).

What is the water potential of the potato tissue?

Question 4 of 8

Two solutions sit in open beakers at the same temperature. Solution A has a water potential of −4 bar. Solution B has a water potential of −9 bar. A piece of plant tissue at −6 bar is put into each in turn.

Which way does water move in each beaker?

Question 5 of 8

A student leaves one set of potato cores unblotted before the final weighing. Every other core is blotted as the method says.

What effect does that have on the results from the unblotted cores?

Question 6 of 8

A walled plant cell with a solute potential of −10 bar is placed in an open beaker of sucrose solution whose water potential is −4 bar. Water enters the cell until its water potential matches the solution's.

What pressure potential has the cell wall reached by then?

Question 7 of 8

A class repeats the potato investigation but leaves the cores in the solutions for only ten minutes instead of an hour.

What is the most likely effect on the position of the zero crossing?

Question 8 of 8

A student wants to find the water potential of a carrot. They cut six cores, weigh each, and leave them in sodium chloride solutions from 0.0 to 0.6 M at 20 °C. Sodium chloride splits into two ions when it dissolves.

What must the student do differently from the potato method?
