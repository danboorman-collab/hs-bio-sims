Eight questions

Systems Interactions · Topic 8.7

On carrying this investigation out, and on what its results show. Each question is marked as soon as it is answered, and every option is explained.

Question 1 of 8

A class surveys mayfly nymphs above and below a farm outflow on one stream. They take twelve kick samples at each site, at positions chosen from pairs of random numbers.

Why are the positions chosen at random rather than picked by eye?

Question 2 of 8

Twelve counts from one site, sorted, are 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 21 and 29 animals per sample.

What is the median of these counts?

Question 3 of 8

Twelve counts from one site, sorted, are 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 21 and 29 animals per sample.

How wide is the box on a box-and-whisker plot of these counts?

Question 4 of 8

Box-and-whisker plots of two sites give these five numbers. Upstream: 18, 22, 26, 30, 38 nymphs per sample. Downstream: 2, 4, 7, 10, 17.

Which statement about the two sets of twelve samples is correct?

Question 5 of 8

A student draws two box-and-whisker plots whose boxes and whiskers do not overlap, and writes: ‘The difference between the two sites is statistically significant.’

What is wrong with that?

Question 6 of 8

The same class finds far fewer mayfly nymphs downstream of a farm outflow than upstream of it, and wants to claim that the outflow caused the difference.

What would most strengthen that claim?

Question 7 of 8

A box-and-whisker plot of yearly percent changes in bird counts at twenty nature reserves has its whole box above a thick line marking zero.

What does that tell you about the twenty reserves?

Question 8 of 8

Ecologists survey a seagrass bed before and after a storm, taking fifteen quadrats each time. Before: median 42 shoots per quadrat, quartiles 36 and 50, whiskers 28 and 61. After: median 24, quartiles 19 and 31, whiskers 11 and 44.

Which statement do the two plots support?
