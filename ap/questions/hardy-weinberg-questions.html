Eight questions

Evolution · Topic 7.5

On carrying this investigation out, and on what its results show. Each question is marked as soon as it is answered, and every option is explained.

Question 1 of 8

A census of a beetle population finds 320 individuals with the genotype RR, 480 with Rr and 200 with rr.

What is the frequency of the R allele in this population?

Question 2 of 8

In a population at Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, the frequency of a recessive allele is 0.30.

What proportion of the population is expected to be heterozygous?

Question 3 of 8

A census of 500 snails found 150 with the genotype BB, 200 Bb and 150 bb. p works out at 0.50.

How many heterozygous snails does Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium predict?

Question 4 of 8

A chi-square test compares a census against the counts Hardy–Weinberg predicts. The population was sorted into three genotype classes.

How many degrees of freedom does the test have, and what is the critical value at p = 0.05?

Question 5 of 8

For the snail census, chi-square came to 20.0 against a critical value of 5.99, and the shortfall falls on the heterozygotes: 200 counted where 250 are predicted.

What does this result establish?

Question 6 of 8

A population of plants has been self-pollinating for many generations. A census finds many more homozygotes and far fewer heterozygotes than Hardy–Weinberg predicts, and the allele frequencies are unchanged from ten years ago.

Which condition of the model does this population break?

Question 7 of 8

In a bird population at Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, 16% of the birds show a recessive plumage pattern.

What proportion of the birds carry the recessive allele without showing the pattern?

Question 8 of 8

Conservation workers census a population of 90 frogs at a pond and find 9 with the genotype SS, 42 with Ss and 39 with ss. A chi-square test against the Hardy–Weinberg expectation gives 0.23.

Using a critical value of 5.99, what do the workers conclude?
