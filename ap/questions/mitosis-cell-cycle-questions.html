Eight questions

Information Storage and Transmission · Topic 4.6

On carrying this investigation out, and on what its results show. Each question is marked as soon as it is answered, and every option is explained.

Question 1 of 8

A root tip is stained, squashed on a slide, and 200 cells are scored for the phase each is in.

Why is the region just behind the tip examined rather than further up the root?

Question 2 of 8

Of 250 cells scored on one slide, 20 were in metaphase.

What percentage of the cells were in metaphase?

Question 3 of 8

On a slide of onion root tip, 8.0% of the cells were in metaphase. The whole cell cycle in these cells takes 20 hours.

How long does metaphase last?

Question 4 of 8

A student scores 200 cells and finds 4 in anaphase. A second student scores 20 cells from the same slide and finds 1 in anaphase.

Why is the first student's figure the more trustworthy?

Question 5 of 8

A student scores only the cells whose phase can be told at a glance, and leaves out the ones that are hard to call.

What effect does that have on the times worked out?

Question 6 of 8

A tissue sample is treated with a drug and then scored. Far more of its cells are in metaphase than in an untreated sample of the same tissue, and the other mitotic phases hold fewer cells than before.

What does the drug most likely do?

Question 7 of 8

Two slides of the same kind of tissue are scored. On the first, 6% of cells are in mitosis. On the second, taken from a tumor, 24% are.

What does the difference suggest?

Question 8 of 8

A biologist scores 400 cells from a growing fish fin and finds 352 in interphase, 28 in prophase, 8 in metaphase, 4 in anaphase and 8 in telophase. The cycle in this tissue takes 18 hours.

How long does the fin's interphase last?
