Eight questions

Evolution · Topic 7.9

On carrying this investigation out, and on what its results show. Each question is marked as soon as it is answered, and every option is explained.

Question 1 of 8

A researcher compares the same gene in five species rather than comparing five different genes.

Why does the gene have to be the same one in every species?

Question 2 of 8

Two species' versions of a protein are 92.0% identical over 450 aligned amino acid positions.

At how many positions do the two sequences differ?

Question 3 of 8

Four species are compared. Species W and X share 97% of a sequence, W and Y share 84%, W and Z share 71%, and X and Z share 70%.

Which two species share the most recent common ancestor?

Question 4 of 8

A cladogram is built from sequence comparisons of five animals. A student points at two species that meet at a node near the right-hand end of the diagram.

What does that node represent?

Question 5 of 8

Five species are compared and one of them shares less of the sequence with each of the other four than any of those four share with each other.

What is that species called, and what is it for?

Question 6 of 8

A student compares a gene in several mammals and finds that two species with very similar body shapes have quite different sequences, while two that look nothing alike have very similar ones.

What is the most likely explanation?

Question 7 of 8

A researcher knows that a certain protein accumulates differences at a roughly steady rate over long periods, and that two species in a group are known from fossils to have separated 60 million years ago. Their sequences differ at 30 positions.

What can the researcher now do that the sequences alone did not allow?

Question 8 of 8

Three plant species are compared at the same gene. A and B are 94% identical, A and C are 79% identical, and B and C are 80% identical.

Which branching order do these figures support?
