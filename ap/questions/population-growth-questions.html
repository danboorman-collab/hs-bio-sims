Eight questions

Systems Interactions · Topic 8.4

On carrying this investigation out, and on what its results show. Each question is marked as soon as it is answered, and every option is explained.

Question 1 of 8

Yeast is grown in a sealed flask of sugar solution and sampled every two hours for sixteen hours. Each sample is diluted and the cells counted under a microscope.

Why is the flask swirled before each sample is taken?

Question 2 of 8

Over the first six hours in a flask, a yeast population rose from 0.4 to 3.2 million cells per mL.

What is the growth constant r over that stretch, if the natural log of 2 is 0.693?

Question 3 of 8

A population of 300 water fleas is growing with r = 0.12 per day in a tank whose carrying capacity is 1200.

How fast is the population growing, using dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K?

Question 4 of 8

A student plots the natural log of a yeast count against time and finds the points lie on a straight line for the first eight hours and then bend away below it.

What does the bend show?

Question 5 of 8

Two flasks of yeast are set up with the same starting number of cells. One holds twice as much sugar as the other. Everything else is the same.

What difference would you expect between the two growth curves?

Question 6 of 8

A yeast population in a sealed flask reaches 17 million cells per mL and stops rising.

What is happening at that point?

Question 7 of 8

A field population of rabbits has grown for several years and now sits close to the carrying capacity of its field. A disease kills three quarters of them in one summer.

What happens to the growth rate of the survivors?

Question 8 of 8

Counts of a duckweed population on a pond, in fronds per square meter, are 20, 41, 82, 160, 300, 470, 580, 620 and 630 over sixteen weeks.

What is the carrying capacity of this pond, as these counts show it?
