Design the investigation

Energetics · Topic 2.7

Six potato cores are about to go into six sucrose solutions. Five decisions first. Choose one answer in each, then submit once.

What this experiment is about

Water moves across a cell's membrane on its own, from where water is more concentrated to where it is less so. Put a piece of potato into a weak sugar solution and water moves in, so the potato gains mass; put it into a strong one and water moves out, so it loses mass. Somewhere between the two is a solution where nothing moves either way. The goal: find that solution, and use it to work out a property of the potato called its water potential.

Decision 1 of 5

Testable question

A testable question names what will be measured and what result would answer it. ‘Is a cell hypertonic?’ is incomplete until it says hypertonic to what.

Decision 2 of 5

Dependent variable — the measurement

The cores are not all the same size, so a change in grams means more for a small core than a large one. Comparing the change with what the core started at fixes that.

What is calculated from each core's two masses?

Controlled variables — fair testing

Each of these changes how much water moves. Any one left free would be a second explanation for a difference between the solutions.

Decision 4 of 5

Reducing measurement error

Measurement error is anything that makes a reading differ from the true value. Liquid clinging to the outside of a core is weighed as though it were part of it.

Why is each core blotted before its final mass is taken?

Experimental design — choosing the range

The answer is read where the line crosses zero, so the concentrations tested have to put points on both sides of zero for that crossing to lie inside the data.
