Design the investigation

Information Storage and Transmission · Topic 4.6

A root tip has been stained and squashed, and 200 cells are about to be scored. Five decisions first. Choose one answer in each, then submit once.

What this experiment is about

A cell that is going to divide passes through the same stages in the same order every time: a long stage of growing and copying its DNA, then four shorter stages in which the copies are pulled apart and the cell splits in two. In a root tip, thousands of cells are doing this at once and each is at its own point in the sequence. Killing and staining them freezes every cell where it was. The goal: use a count of those frozen cells to work out how long each stage lasts.

Decision 1 of 5

The principle behind the method

Every cell was killed at the same moment, so a stage that lasts longer catches more cells in it. That is what lets a count stand for a length of time.

Why does the share of cells in a phase give the share of the cycle that phase takes?

Decision 2 of 5

Sampling site

The sample has to come from where the thing you are measuring is happening. Cells that have stopped dividing tell you nothing about how long dividing takes.

Which part of the root is examined?

Decision 3 of 5

Replicates and sample size

Sample size is how many cells you score. The rarest stage holds only a few cells in a hundred, so a small count swings wildly from one field of view to the next.

Why are 200 cells scored rather than 20?

Decision 4 of 5

Avoiding bias

Bias is anything that lets a choice about what to record change the result. Skipping the cells that are hard to call is such a choice.

Why is every cell in a chosen field scored, rather than only the ones that are clearly in a phase?

Decision 5 of 5

Data brought in from elsewhere

The counts give each stage as a share of the cycle. A share has to be taken of something before it becomes a time, and this method cannot measure that something.

The cycle length has to be known from elsewhere. Why?
