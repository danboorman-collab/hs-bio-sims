Read the two sites off a box plot

Median, quartiles, the five-number summary, and what a box plot can and cannot say.

Twelve counts at each site are too many to read as a list. Five numbers are enough to draw the whole set: the smallest, the lower quartile, the median, the upper quartile and the largest.

You will work the upstream site by hand. The downstream site has been worked the same way.

Step 1 — the median

The median is the middle value. With twelve counts there is no single middle one, so the median is halfway between the sixth and the seventh.

Why this step: Twelve counts are too many to hold in the head at once. The median is the first of five numbers that stand for the whole set, and it is the one the two sites are compared on. It is the middle value, so a single very large count cannot drag it about the way it would drag a mean.

Step 2 — the lower quartile

A quartile is the middle of each half. The lower half is positions 1 to 6, so its middle lies between the third and the fourth counts.

Why this step: The median says where the middle is but nothing about how spread out the samples were. The quartiles are the edges of the box, and the box holds the middle half of the samples. This is its lower edge.

Step 3 — the upper quartile

The upper half is positions 7 to 12, so its middle lies between the ninth and the tenth counts.

Why this step: And this is the box's upper edge, found the same way from the other half. With both quartiles the box can be drawn, and a quarter of the samples lie above it.

Step 4 — the interquartile range

The box runs from the lower quartile to the upper quartile, so the middle half of the samples lies inside it. How wide that box is says how spread out the middle half was.

Why this step: How wide the box is says how much the stream bed varies from one patch to the next. It is worth having as a number because the two sites are compared on their spread as well as on their middles.

Step 7 — what would test it

What would have to be drawn to test whether the two sites really differ?

Upstream: 18, 22, 26, 30, 38. Downstream: 2, 4, 7, 10, 17. The downstream median is 19 nymphs lower, and the two sets of samples share no value at all. The box plots describe that difference. They do not test it, and saying so is part of reading them correctly.
