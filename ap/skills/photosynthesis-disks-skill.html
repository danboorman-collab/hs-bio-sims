A time turned into a rate

ET50, the reciprocal that turns it into a rate, and where the light stops being the limiting factor.

A disk rises when the oxygen made inside it fills the air spaces the vacuum emptied. ET50 is the time at which half the disks have risen.

ET50 is a time, and a time runs the opposite way to a rate: the faster the photosynthesis, the shorter the wait. Its reciprocal does not.

Step 1 — a rate from a time

If half the disks rise in 10 minutes, half a set rises per 10 minutes, which is 0.1 of a set per minute. Dividing 1 by the time gives the rate.

Why this step: ET50 is a time, and a time runs the opposite way to a rate: the faster the photosynthesis, the shorter the wait. Plotted as it stands it would slope the wrong way. Its reciprocal rises as the rate rises, which is what can be read as a rate.

rate = 1 ÷ ET50

Step 4 — how much faster

A percent change says how large a rise is compared with what it started from.

Why this step: A rise of 0.035 per minute means nothing until it is compared with what it started from. A percentage does that, and it is what makes the steep part of the curve and the flat part comparable: the same absolute rise is a large change at one end and a small one at the other.

percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100

What does the dark control establish?

The rate rises steeply from 50 to 200 µmol photons m−² s−¹ and then levels off. Over the steep stretch, light is the factor holding the rate down. Past it, something else is, and the disks alone cannot say what. The dark control is what lets any of it be put down to photosynthesis.
