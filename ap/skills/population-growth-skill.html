From a curve to r and K

The straight stretch on a log scale, the growth constant it gives, and the braking term.

Unchecked growth multiplies the population by the same factor in every equal stretch of time. That is a curve on an ordinary scale and a straight line on a log scale, which is why the counts are plotted both ways.

Where the log plot stops being straight is where the growth stopped being unchecked.

Step 2 — the growth constant

A population growing without check multiplies by e to the power rt. Doubling means that factor is 2, so r is the natural log of 2 divided by the time the doubling took.

r = ln 2 ÷ doubling time

Step 3 — the carrying capacity

The last three counts are within half a million of one another. The value the curve is settling on is the most cells this flask can support: its carrying capacity, K.

Step 4 — the growth rate early on

The logistic equation multiplies the unchecked growth rate by a braking term, (K − N) / K. When N is small the brake is near 1 and barely slows anything.

dN/dt = rN × (K − N) ÷ K

Which of these is most likely to be setting K here?

r = 0.347 per hour, K ≈ 17 million cells/mL. r comes from the straight stretch of the log plot, where nothing is yet holding the yeast back. K comes from the ordinary plot, where everything is. The braking term (K − N) / K is what turns the first into the second.
