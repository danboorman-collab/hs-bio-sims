Choose the conclusion

A conclusion says what the results show. It is not a summary of what you did.

First, a conclusion that does the job — Water insects in two ponds

Two ponds were swept with the same net for the same time, and the water insects caught were identified and counted.

Both ponds gave 60 insects of 4 species, so counting species cannot separate them. In pond A the insects are shared evenly between the four, 14 to 16 of each, while in pond B one species accounts for 54 of the 60. Pond A is therefore the more diverse. The survey does not show which pond is cleaner, because only the insects were counted.

  • Which one, and on what. counting species cannot separate them ... Pond A is therefore the more diverse — The conclusion says why the obvious measure fails before it says what decides.
  • Figures from both. 4 species and 60 insects in each, 14 to 16 of each against 54 of the 60 — Figures from both ponds, so the reader can see the difference for themselves.
  • What the survey does not show. only the insects were counted — A net full of insects says nothing about the water, so no claim is made about it.

Your own conclusion has to do those same three things, and it has to use figures from both of the places you surveyed.

What that conclusion had to do

  • Which one, and on what. Which of the two the survey picks out, and the measure that picks it.
  • Figures from both. Numbers from each of them, so a reader can see the difference.
  • What the survey does not show. The questions this survey was never going to answer.
