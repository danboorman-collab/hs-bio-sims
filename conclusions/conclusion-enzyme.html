Choose the conclusion

A conclusion says what the results show. It is not a summary of what you did.

How to write a conclusion

Three steps, in this order. They are the same three whatever you investigated.

  1. State the overall trend.Say what happened to the thing you measured as the thing you changed went up. One sentence, in plain words, and covering the whole range rather than the start of it.
  2. Give the data that evidences it.Quote figures from your own results, usually one from each end of the range. A direction with no numbers behind it is an opinion, and a reader cannot check it.
  3. Point out any exceptions.Name any reading that does not follow the trend, say where it is, and say what it does instead. An exception left out quietly is the one thing a reader cannot forgive.

Now do those three things for your own results. Only one of the three below does all of them, and it has to fit every reading you took.

Now your own results

Your means were 5 bubbles in one minute at 10 °C, 10 at 20 °C, 18 at 30 °C, 25 at 40 °C and 7 at 50 °C. Every temperature was run three times, and the three runs at 50 °C agreed with each other.

Which of these is a conclusion, and one your own results support?

Pick one. You get one go at it, and then all three are explained.

The three steps again

  1. State the overall trend.
  2. Give the data that evidences it.
