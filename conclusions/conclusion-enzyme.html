Choose the conclusion

A conclusion says what the results show. It is not a summary of what you did.

First, a conclusion that does the job — Seedlings at five temperatures

Seedlings of the same kind were grown at five temperatures and their height measured after two weeks.

As the temperature rose from 10 °C to 25 °C the seedlings grew taller, from 4 cm at 10 °C to 11 cm at 25 °C. Above 25 °C they grew less well, reaching only 6 cm at 30 °C. So these seedlings grow tallest at about 25 °C. The results do not show what happens above 30 °C, because no higher temperature was tested.

  • What the results show. taller as the temperature rose, then less well above 25 °C — The height rises and then falls, so the conclusion says both halves, not just the rise.
  • A figure from them. 4 cm, 11 cm and 6 cm — Three heights taken straight from the results, so the reader can see the size of the change.
  • What they do not show. nothing above 30 °C was tested — 30 °C was the hottest temperature tried, so the results say nothing about anything hotter.

Your own conclusion has to do those same three things, and it has to fit every one of your readings, not just the first few.
