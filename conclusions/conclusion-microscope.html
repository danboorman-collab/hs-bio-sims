Choose the conclusion

A conclusion says what the results show. It is not a summary of what you did.

How to write a conclusion

Three steps, in this order. They are the same three whatever you investigated.

  1. State the answer the question asked for.Give the number, and its units if it has any. With a calculation the working usually comes first and the number follows it, but the number has to be there and has to be plainly the answer.
  2. Give the data that evidences it.Show the figures it came from and the steps between them, so a reader can check the arithmetic instead of trusting it.
  3. Point out any exceptions.Name any reading that does not fit the others and say what you did about it. An answer that is an estimate rather than a count, or that carries no units where a reader would expect some, belongs here too.

Now do those three things for your own figures. Only one of the three below does all of them, and it has to end in the number the question asked for.

Now your own results

You were asked how many times larger than the real cell your drawing is. You drew a row of 8 cells, and the row you drew measures 60 mm long. A real cell of this kind is 0.3 mm long.

Which of these is a conclusion, and one your own figures support?

Pick one. You get one go at it, and then all three are explained.

The three steps again

  1. State the answer the question asked for.
  2. Give the data that evidences it.
  3. Point out any exceptions.
