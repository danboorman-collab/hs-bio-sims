Choose the conclusion

A conclusion says what the results show. It is not a summary of what you did.

First, a conclusion that does the job — Bags of dialysis tubing

Five bags of dialysis tubing, each holding the same sucrose solution, were left for 30 minutes in sucrose solutions of five strengths, then weighed.

The bags in the weaker solutions gained mass and those in the stronger ones lost it: +18% in pure water and −15% in 25% sucrose. The line of best fit crosses zero at about 13.5% sucrose, so a solution of about that strength is as concentrated as the solution inside the bags. The results do not show what happens above 25% sucrose, because no stronger solution was tested.

  • What the results show. gained mass ... lost it ... crosses zero at about 13.5% sucrose — The direction changes partway along, so the conclusion says where it changes, not just which way it goes.
  • A figure from them. +18%, −15% and 13.5% — The two ends and the crossing point, all read off the graph that was drawn.
  • What they do not show. no stronger solution was tested — 25% sucrose was the strongest tried, so nothing can be said about a stronger one.

Your own conclusion has to do those same three things, and it has to fit every one of your readings, not just the first few.

What that conclusion had to do

  • What the results show. How the thing that was changed affected the thing that was measured.
  • A figure from them. A number read off the results, not just a direction.
  • What they do not show. The edge of what was tested, said out loud.
