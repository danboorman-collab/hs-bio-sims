Choose the conclusion

A conclusion says what the results show. It is not a summary of what you did.

First, a conclusion that does the job — Nitrogen on a wheat crop

Plots of wheat were given five amounts of nitrogen fertilizer, and the grain from each plot was weighed at harvest.

As the nitrogen rose from 0 to 120 kg/ha the yield rose steadily, from 4.0 to 8.0 t/ha. From 120 to 160 kg/ha the yield stayed at 8.0 t/ha. So nitrogen was holding the yield back up to 120 kg/ha, and above that something else was. The results do not show what that other thing is, because only the nitrogen was changed.

  • What the results show. rose steadily ... then stayed at 8.0 t/ha — The line levels off, and the conclusion says where it levels off rather than treating the whole graph as one rise.
  • A figure from them. 0 to 120 kg/ha, 4.0 to 8.0 t/ha — The amounts and the yields, so the reader can see where the change stops.
  • What they do not show. only the nitrogen was changed — Something else holds the yield back above 120 kg/ha, but this investigation cannot say what, because it changed nothing else.

Your own conclusion has to do those same three things, and it has to fit every one of your readings, not just the first few.

Pick one. You get one go at it, and then all three are explained.

What that conclusion had to do

  • What the results show. How the thing that was changed affected the thing that was measured.
  • A figure from them. A number read off the results, not just a direction.
  • What they do not show. The edge of what was tested, said out loud.
