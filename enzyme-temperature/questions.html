Enzymes and temperature

Eight questions on the practical and on what its results show.

Question 1 of 8

A student sets a water bath to 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 °C in turn and counts the bubbles released in one minute at each one. Which variable belongs on the horizontal axis of the graph?

Question 2 of 8

In this practical the same volume of hydrogen peroxide is used at every temperature. Why does that volume have to be the same each time?

Question 3 of 8

The bubbles are counted three times at every temperature and a mean is worked out. What does taking three readings achieve that one reading would not?

Question 4 of 8

An enzyme breaks hydrogen peroxide down, and the reaction is followed by counting the bubbles that come off. What are those bubbles made of?

Question 5 of 8

A graph of the results climbs from 10 °C to a highest point at 40 °C and then falls steeply. What does the temperature at that highest point tell you about the enzyme?

Question 6 of 8

Above 40 °C the rate does not level off but falls steeply. What is happening to the enzyme over that part of the curve?

Question 7 of 8

A student heats the mixture to 60 °C, then cools it back to 40 °C and counts again. The rate stays very low. Why does cooling not bring it back up?

Question 8 of 8

The test tube stands in a beaker of water rather than being warmed on the hotplate itself. What does the water around the tube do?
