Required practical · High School Biology

Comparing two forests

Two forests hold 200 trees of 5 species each. Which is the more biodiverse? Seven stages, in the order a student works through them — watch, plan, survey, judge, conclude, answer.

What we count
Every tree on a set route, tallied against its species
How many
200 trees in each forest, the same number in both
What we compare
How many species, and how the trees are shared between them
1. Watch the method2. Plan the survey3. Collect the results4. Watch how to judge a conclusion5. Judge the conclusions6. Choose the conclusion7. Answer eight questions

Watch the method

The route, the identification key, and why the same number of trees is counted in each forest.

Stage 2Question · Four decisions

Plan the survey

Four things to decide before either forest is walked. One check, then all four are explained.

Stage 3Simulation · Two forests, 200 trees each

Collect the results

Walk the same route in each forest and watch the tally build against the five species.

Stage 4Video · 89 seconds

Watch how to judge a conclusion

Why species richness cannot separate these two forests, what evenness adds, and two conclusions the data does not support.

Stage 5Simulation · Three conclusions, one supported

Judge the conclusions

Three statements about the two forests. Decide which the survey supports, and say what is wrong with the others.

Stage 6Question · A worked example, then one choice

Choose the conclusion

What does the survey show? A conclusion from another investigation, with what makes it good, and then three for your own.

Stage 7Questions · Eight items

Answer eight questions

On carrying the survey out, and on what its figures show.

Nothing here is marked behind the scenes. TimeBack cannot see inside a simulation, so the simulations are the practical and what a student is assessed on is the eight questions at the end, together with the checkpoints in the lessons this practical belongs to.

The readings are the same for every student, because a marked question needs one right answer. The analysis skill this practical teaches is forming a conclusion the data supports, and no two of the six practicals teach the same one.
