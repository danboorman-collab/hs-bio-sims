Required practical · High School Biology

Cells under the microscope

How much larger than the real cell is a drawing of it? Seven stages, in the order a student works through them — watch, set up, measure, calculate, conclude, answer.

What we prepare
A slide of onion epidermis, one cell thick, stained with iodine
What we measure
The row of cells as drawn, and the real length of one cell
What we work out
The magnification of the drawing, which carries no units
1. Watch the method2. Set out the equipment3. Collect the results4. Watch how to calculate magnification5. Work out the magnification6. Choose the conclusion7. Answer eight questions
Stage 1Video · 86 seconds

Watch the method

Peeling the epidermis, staining it, and lowering the coverslip from one edge so no air is trapped under it.

Stage 2Simulation · Fourteen pieces on the bench

Set out the equipment

Choose every piece the method needs. One submission, then every piece is marked and explained.

Stage 3Simulation · Count the cells, measure the row

Collect the results

Count the cells across the field of view, then measure the row you drew against a ruler.

Stage 4Video · 86 seconds

Watch how to calculate magnification

Image size over actual size, the same units on both, and why the answer comes out with no units at all.

Stage 5Simulation · Three steps, units checked

Work out the magnification

Divide the row down to one cell, divide by the real cell, and say what units the answer carries.

Stage 6Question · Three steps, then one choice

Choose the conclusion

What do your figures show? The three steps a conclusion takes, your own figures back on the page, and three conclusions to choose between.

Stage 7Questions · Eight items

Answer eight questions

On making the slide, and on reading what it shows.

Nothing here is marked behind the scenes. TimeBack cannot see inside a simulation, so the simulations are the practical and what a student is assessed on is the eight questions at the end, together with the checkpoints in the lessons this practical belongs to.

The readings are the same for every student, because a marked question needs one right answer. The analysis skill this practical teaches is calculating magnification, and no two of the six practicals teach the same one.
