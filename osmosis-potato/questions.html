Osmosis: potato and sugar solution

Eight questions on the practical and on what its results show.

Question 1 of 8

A student leaves a cylinder of potato in each of five sucrose solutions for 30 minutes and works out how much mass each one gained or lost. Which variable belongs on the horizontal axis of the graph?

Question 2 of 8

Every cylinder is blotted dry on a paper towel before it is weighed, both at the start and at the end. Why does that matter?

Question 3 of 8

All five cylinders are cut from the same potato with the same cork borer and trimmed to the same length. Why must they be the same size as each other?

Question 4 of 8

A cylinder left in pure water gains mass. What has moved, and which way?

Question 5 of 8

A cylinder left in 20% sucrose loses mass. What has happened to it?

Question 6 of 8

A graph of the results is a straight line that crosses zero at about 10% sucrose. What does that concentration tell you about the potato?

Question 7 of 8

Using that same graph, which crosses zero at about 10% sucrose, what would happen to a cylinder left in 5% sucrose?

Question 8 of 8

The results are worked out as a percentage change in mass rather than as a change in grams. What does using a percentage allow?
