Plan the survey

Four decisions to make before either forest is walked. One check, then all four are explained.

What this experiment is about

Two woodlands can hold the same number of trees and still be very different places: one may be nearly all a single species, the other an even mixture of several. Biodiversity is a way of putting a number on that difference. The goal: survey both forests the same way and decide which is the more biodiverse.

The problem. Two forests are to be compared, so the only thing that may differ between the two surveys is the forest itself.

Anything else that changes — how many trees are counted, which way the route runs, who does the identifying — turns up in the figures as if it were a difference between the forests, and nothing in the table would say otherwise.
