Comparing two forests

Eight questions on the survey and on what its figures show.

Question 1 of 8

Two forests are being compared for biodiversity, and 200 trees are counted in each one. Why is the same number counted in both?

Question 2 of 8

A forest survey records 200 trees belonging to 5 species. What is the species richness of that forest?

Question 3 of 8

A mixed forest holds 41, 39, 38, 42 and 40 trees of its 5 species. A plantation holds 196, 1, 1, 1 and 1. What does evenness tell you that species richness does not?

Question 4 of 8

A mixed forest and a plantation each hold 200 trees of 5 species. In the mixed forest the counts run from 38 to 42; in the plantation one species has 196. Which is the more biodiverse, and why?

Question 5 of 8

A survey counts and identifies 200 trees in each of two forests and nothing else. Which of these can the survey not say?

Question 6 of 8

During a forest survey a student meets a tree they cannot name. What should they do with it?

Question 7 of 8

Two forests are surveyed on the same day using the same identification key. Why does it matter that the key is the same one?

Question 8 of 8

In a plantation, four of the five species have just one tree each out of 200. A student says the plantation therefore has a species richness of 1. Why is that wrong?
