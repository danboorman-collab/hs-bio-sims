What this experiment is about

A drawing of a cell is far bigger than the cell itself, and how much bigger is called the magnification. If you know the real width of a row of cells and the width of your drawing of it, you can work that number out. The goal: prepare a slide of onion cells, draw what you see, and calculate how many times larger the drawing is.

The method. The skin lining the inner curve of an onion scale is one cell thick, which is thin enough to see through. Peeling a piece of it off and mounting it on a slide gives a view of cells with nothing in front of them.

The peel is almost colorless, so one drop of iodine stain is added to give the structures inside the cells some color.

The coverslip goes on last, and it goes on from one edge rather than flat, so the glass pushes the air out ahead of itself instead of trapping it over the specimen.
