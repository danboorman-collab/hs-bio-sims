Onion cells under the microscope

Eight questions on making the slide and on reading what it shows.

Question 1 of 8

To make a slide of onion cells, a student peels the skin from the inner curve of an onion scale. Why is that piece of skin used rather than a slice of the onion?

Question 2 of 8

One drop of iodine is added to the onion peel before the coverslip goes on. What does the iodine do?

Question 3 of 8

When mounting a slide, the coverslip is stood on one edge and lowered slowly rather than dropped flat. What does lowering it that way avoid?

Question 4 of 8

A student is focusing a slide with the lowest-power objective. Why is the lens moved away from the slide while focusing, and not toward it?

Question 5 of 8

A drawing of a cell measures 7.5 mm across, and the real cell is 0.3 mm across. What is the magnification of the drawing?

Question 6 of 8

A student draws a row of 8 cells and the row measures 60 mm. A real cell is 0.3 mm long. Why is 60 divided by 8 before anything else is done?

Question 7 of 8

A student works out that a drawing is 25 times the size of the real cell and writes the answer as ×25 mm. What is wrong with writing it that way?

Question 8 of 8

Two microscopes both magnify ×400, but one shows two structures as separate where the other shows a single blur. What does that difference show?
