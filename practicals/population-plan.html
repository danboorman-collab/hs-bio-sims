Plan the survey

Four decisions to make before anyone walks into the field. One check, then all four are explained.

What this experiment is about

Counting every plant of one species in a whole field is impossible, so instead you count them inside a few small squares and scale the answer up. That only works if the squares stand in fairly for the field. The goal: estimate how many plants of one species the field holds, and know how far the estimate can be trusted.

The problem. The field holds far more plants of this species than anyone could count one at a time. So a sample is counted instead: a few small squares of known area, scaled up to the whole field.

That only works if the squares stand in fairly for the field. Every decision below is about whether they do.
