Estimating a population with quadrats

Eight questions on the survey and on what its figures show.

Question 1 of 8

A student is estimating how many plants of one species grow in a field, using ten quadrats. Why are the quadrats put down at coordinates taken from random numbers?

Question 2 of 8

A pair of random numbers lands a quadrat on a patch of bare ground with none of the chosen species in it. What should the student record?

Question 3 of 8

Ten quadrats in a field give counts of 6, 4, 7, 5, 6, 3, 7, 5, 8 and 9 plants. What is the mean number of plants per quadrat?

Question 4 of 8

Each quadrat in a field survey is a square of side 0.5 m, and the field measures 800 m². How many quadrat areas fit into the field?

Question 5 of 8

Ten quadrats give a mean of 6 plants each, 3,200 quadrat areas fit into the field, and the student writes down 19,200 plants. How should that figure be described?

Question 6 of 8

A student puts all ten quadrats in the greenest part of a field, where the plants grow most thickly. Which way does the estimate go, and why?

Question 7 of 8

A student suggests using two quadrats instead of ten, since two counts still give a mean. What does the field lose by it?

Question 8 of 8

Two students survey the same field. One counts every plant inside the frame, the other counts only the chosen species. Whose estimate answers the question the survey asked, and why?
